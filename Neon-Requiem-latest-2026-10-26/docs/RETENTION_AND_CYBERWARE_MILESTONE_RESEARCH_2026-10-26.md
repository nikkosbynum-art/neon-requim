# Retention and Cyberware Milestone Research — 2026-10-26

**Status: UNAPPROVED LOCAL MILESTONE CANDIDATE.** This research brief is planning material only. It does not add canon, authorize implementation, choose balance values, or claim remote Notion/Drive synchronization.

## Candidate milestone

### District Operations and Cyberware Buildcraft — post-1.0 candidate

Explore a repeatable, non-timed district challenge that uses the existing automatic-combat model and awards deterministic cyberware upgrade materials. Players would configure capacity-limited cyberware loadouts that create meaningful team specializations, then use earned materials to improve those implants. Specific operation rules, implant identities, capacity, effects, costs, reward tables, and unlocks remain unapproved.

This adapts two patterns without copying their setting or content:

- **Endless Frontier 2:** rebirth converts a run's progress into permanent growth; boss dungeons provide materials for relic upgrades; hero traits and positions encourage team planning. Its official listings also describe daily/seasonal rankings and convenience systems. Neon Requiem's candidate borrows the repeatable challenge → deterministic material → permanent build progression loop, not its monetization, schedule pressure, or online ranking requirements.
- **Endless Frontier:** broad roster evolution and side activities (including a team-building tower and dungeon bosses feeding artifact progression) create goals beyond ordinary stage pushing. Its official listing also advertises offline progress and guild/PvP features. These are inspiration references, not an instruction to add social infrastructure.
- **Cyberpunk 2077:** the official Update 2.0 explanation treats cyberware as part of a build and uses capacity to make implant choices meaningful. Its build-planner article emphasizes testing distinct playstyle combinations before committing. The candidate translates that into legible team specialization under automatic combat; it does not copy branded implants, terminology, perks, or numerical rules.

## Research implications for Neon Requiem

1. Keep several time horizons: short-session spend/claim feedback, medium-term district or boss milestones, and long-term Reboot/research/evolution progression. Existing approved systems already cover much of this; this candidate is a possible bridge, not a reason to replace them.
2. Make progression visibly legible. The idle-game study reports participants valued flexible progress, visible unlocks/features, and continuous saved progress; it also notes disappointment when accumulated resources yield too little meaningful action. This supports presenting a cyberware upgrade as a clear change in loadout and appearance/effect, rather than adding another opaque currency bar.
3. Preserve player choice without changing combat control. Loadout/build choices can happen before an encounter while targeting and abilities stay automatic. No player-triggered abilities or manual target selection are proposed.
4. Avoid importing daily/seasonal rankings, timed exclusivity, paid acceleration, or gacha from market examples. They conflict with the project's current constraints or add online-service work without a demonstrated need.

## Follow-up evidence review and recommendation — 2026-10-26

### What the references actually support

- The current official **Endless Frontier 2** listing describes a compact set of complementary loops: automatic/offline progress, Rebirth into permanent medals, hero traits and formation, dungeon bosses that pay upgrade materials, and convenience features such as Wave Pull and bulk leveling. The official **Endless Frontier** listing similarly advertises offline autoquests, repeated revival, a large unit/hero collection, relics, and boss dungeons. These are developer-listed feature inventories, not controlled evidence that any single feature causes retention.
- CD PROJEKT RED's Update 2.0 explanation frames cyberware as build-defining equipment with a capacity limit and meaningful trade-offs. Its build-planner article says perks were redesigned toward fewer, more impactful playstyle changes and supports planning/respec outside combat. The transferable lesson is distinct build identity plus reversible experimentation, not copying Cyberpunk's tree, perks, or branded implants.
- Hwang's mixed-methods idle-game thesis reports that participants valued flexible away-progress, visible unlocks/new features, and continuous saved progress. Participants also described accumulated currency without new meaningful outcomes as weak motivation. The evidence is directional: the thesis notes small qualitative samples and limits on longitudinal inference; it does not establish a universal retention formula.

### Recommended changes, ranked

1. **Make every return session explain its progress.** After loading/claiming offline credits, show a compact return report: time away, capped credits gained, the saved holding wave used, and any milestone or purchase newly available. End with one clear optional next goal (for example, upgrade, recruit, push, or Reboot forecast). This strengthens the existing away-progress loop without adding a currency or a login streak. Keep offline claims within their approved credit-only/no-wave-advance rules.
2. **Advance the existing District Operations and Cyberware Buildcraft idea only after the 1.0 combat/economy gates.** Prototype one permanent, replayable operation per completed district, with automatic combat and a deterministic, visible reward. Use the operation to let players test a meaningful cyberware build trade-off. Keep it non-timed and solo; do not alter boss recruitment guarantees or existing first-clear rewards. Reuse an existing material where it remains legible; add at most one new material family only if the balance model demonstrates that reuse cannot serve the specialization loop.
3. **Make cyberware choice change playstyle, not just percentages.** Start with a small set of mutually competing implants and explicit capacity costs; make their effects inspectable before combat and allow swapping or resetting outside combat. Preserve automatic ability activation and targeting. Do not add these to the deferred Research catalog.
4. **Use the approved SPARK/ECHO air lane as character identity, but finish the spatial contract first.** Define how air height relates to fixed depth bands, targeting, attack range, collision, obstacles, regrouping, and ability timing. Keep other characters grounded. Treat aerial advantages and numeric values as hypotheses for playtesting, not assumptions.
5. **Reduce routine input before adding more currencies or modes.** Preserve bulk leveling and clear upgrade/recruit affordability previews; expose a short list of useful next actions. Do not add time-limited events, consecutive-login pressure, gacha, rankings, guild features, or paid acceleration. EF listings include several such systems, but they conflict with the current solo, deterministic, no-gacha scope or would add service complexity.

**Priority recommendation:** Keep items 1 and existing 1.0 readability/economy work in the near-term. Retain items 2–4 as unapproved post-1.0 candidate work, sequenced after combat, ability, and balance validation. The references do not justify expanding the current release scope or adding a new progression currency now.
